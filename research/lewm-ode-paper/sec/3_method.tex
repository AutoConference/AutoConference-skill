\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\method{} is a JEPA-style visual world model whose latent \emph{evolution}
is continuous: we replace the discrete autoregressive next-window map with
an action-conditioned velocity field integrated over a normalized step
interval.
Notation follows the official PushT implementation (history length
$H{=}3$, embedding dimension $D{=}192$, one prediction per window).

\subsection{Problem statement and objective}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $f_\phi$ be the pixel encoder, $p$ and $q$ the representation and
prediction projectors, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SIGReg}}$ the SIGReg
anti-collapse term.
From a history of observations $x_{t-H+1:t}$ and actions $u_{t-H+1:t}$ we
form context latents $Z_t$ and action embeddings $A_t$ (detailed in
\Cref{sec:encoder}).
The \emph{discrete} baseline predictor $g^{\mathrm{AR}}_\theta$ maps
\begin{equation}
  \hat Z_{t+1} = g^{\mathrm{AR}}_\theta(Z_t, A_t).
  \label{eq:prob-ar}
\end{equation}
\method{} instead defines a velocity field $v_\theta$ and integrates
\begin{equation}
  \hat Z_{t+1}
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{ODESolve}\bigl(v_\theta;\, Z_t, A_t;\, \tau{:}0\to 1\bigr),
  \label{eq:prob-ode}
\end{equation}
with piecewise-constant actions on $[0,1]$ (one CEM planning tick).
Both variants are trained to minimize the same JEPA + SIGReg objective
(\Cref{sec:loss}) on identical official PushT windows and evaluated with
the same val-tied CEM starts.
We do \emph{not} redesign the encoder, change SIGReg hyperparameters,
introduce pixel reconstruction, or alter planner costs: any difference is
attributable to how latent state advances between steps.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can continuous integrate-to-next-step dynamics match discrete AR
under matched JEPA training and CEM planning on official PushT data?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Encoder and action embedding}
\label{sec:encoder}

Let $x_{t-H+1:t}$ be a stack of RGB observations at resolution
$224{\times}224$.
A ViT-tiny encoder $f_\phi$ (patch size 14, 12 layers, hidden size 192,
3 attention heads, trained from scratch) maps each frame to a CLS
token.
An MLP projector $p$ with BatchNorm yields latents
\begin{equation}
  z_t \;=\; p\bigl(f_\phi(x_t)\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{D}.
  \label{eq:encode}
\end{equation}
Raw actions $u_t$ (PushT end-effector commands) are mapped by an
embedder $e_\psi$ to
\begin{equation}
  a_t \;=\; e_\psi(u_t)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}.
  \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
Context tensors for one prediction step are the history windows
\begin{equation}
  Z \;=\; (z_{t-H+1},\ldots,z_t),\qquad
  A \;=\; (a_{t-H+1},\ldots,a_t),
\end{equation}
each of shape $(H, D)$ per batch element (implementation uses
$(B,H,D)$).

\subsection{Discrete baseline predictor}
\label{sec:ar}

The discrete baseline is an action-conditioned transformer
(ConditionalBlock / AdaLN-zero) of depth 6, 16 heads, width $D$, MLP
dimension 2048, and dropout 0.1---the same architectural family used in
prior JEPA PushT stacks~\cite{maes2026lewm}:
\begin{equation}
  \hat Z_{t+1}
  \;=\;
  g^{\mathrm{AR}}_\theta(Z, A),
  \label{eq:ar}
\end{equation}
interpreted as the next latent window (same shape as $Z$).
Internally, learnable positional embeddings are added along the
history axis, and actions $A$ condition every block through AdaLN.
A prediction-head MLP $q$ is applied after the predictor for the MSE
term; $q$ is unchanged in our ODE variant.

\subsection{ODE predictor}
\label{sec:ode}

We reinterpret the same transformer capacity as a
\textbf{velocity field} $v_\theta$, followed by a zero-initialized
linear head $W_v\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$ with bias
(adds $D^2{+}D{=}37{,}056$ parameters):
\begin{equation}
  v_\theta(z, a)
  \;=\;
  W_v\,\mathrm{TF}_\theta\bigl(z + \mathrm{PE},\, a\bigr),
  \label{eq:velocity}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{PE}$ is a learnable positional embedding over the
history axis and actions $a$ condition AdaLN as in the AR baseline.
Latent dynamics on a normalized step interval $\tau\in[0,1]$ are
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}\tau}
  \;=\;
  v_\theta\bigl(z(\tau),\, a\bigr),
  \qquad
  z(0) = Z,
  \label{eq:ode}
\end{equation}
with $a$ \emph{held fixed} over the interval (piecewise-constant
action, matching one CEM planning step and the discrete action
schedule).
\textbf{One-step prediction} is the solution at $\tau{=}1$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat Z_{t+1}
  \;=\;
  z(1)
  \;=\;
  Z + \int_{0}^{1} v_\theta\bigl(z(\tau),\, a\bigr)\, \mathrm{d}\tau.
  \label{eq:onestep}
\end{equation}
We approximate the integral with fixed-step classical RK4 using
$N{=}4$ substeps ($h{=}1/N$); no \texttt{torchdiffeq} dependency is
required.
One RK4 substep is
\begin{align}
  k_1 &= v_\theta(z,a), \nonumber\\
  k_2 &= v_\theta(z + \tfrac{h}{2}k_1,\, a), \nonumber\\
  k_3 &= v_\theta(z + \tfrac{h}{2}k_2,\, a), \nonumber\\
  k_4 &= v_\theta(z + h\,k_3,\, a), \nonumber\\
  z &\leftarrow z + \tfrac{h}{6}(k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4),
  \label{eq:rk4}
\end{align}
repeated $N$ times from $z(0)=Z$ to obtain $z(1)$.
Zero-init of $W_v$ yields $v\approx 0$ at initialization, hence
$z(1)\approx z(0)$ (stable residual ODE), analogous to AdaLN-zero
initialization in the discrete predictor.

\begin{remark}[Discrete interface, continuous internals]
The planner still sees a discrete next-window map
$Z\mapsto\hat Z_{t+1}$.
Continuity lives inside the map via RK4; we do not change CEM's
horizon, action blocking, or cost.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Training objective (JEPA + SIGReg)}
\label{sec:loss}

The total loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  \;=\;
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}}
  \;+\;
  \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SIGReg}},
  \qquad
  \lambda = 0.09,
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where we write $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}}$ for the next-embedding
prediction term (also called $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}$ in logs).
Concretely, if $q$ is the prediction projector and stop-grad targets
are $\mathrm{sg}[p(f_\phi(x_{t+1}))]$ (history-aligned),
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}}
  \;=\;
  \bigl\|
    q(\hat Z_{t+1}) - \mathrm{sg}\bigl[p(f_\phi(x^{\mathrm{tgt}}))\bigr]
  \bigr\|_2^{2},
  \label{eq:jepa}
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SIGReg}}$ is the sketched Epps--Pulley
statistic over $M{=}1024$ random projections and 17 quadrature knots
\cite{balestriero2025lejepa,maes2026lewm}.
Writing $P$ for projected embedding coordinates and $t_k$ for
quadrature nodes, SIGReg accumulates a discrepancy between the
empirical characteristic function of $P$ and that of
$\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ across random directions; we use the reference
implementation unchanged (weight $\lambda{=}0.09$).
Only the predictor internals differ between discrete and ODE runs;
encoder, projectors, SIGReg, and optimizer are shared.

\subsection{Planning interface (CEM)}
\label{sec:cem}

At evaluation, Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) samples action sequences,
rolls out the predictor in latent space from encoded pixels, and
selects the plan minimizing distance to a goal embedding (official
PushT CEM config: 300 samples, 30 CEM iterations, top-$k{=}30$,
horizon / receding horizon / action block $=5$, goal offset 25
steps, max episode length 100).
For the ODE model, each rollout step applies
\eqref{eq:onestep}--\eqref{eq:rk4} instead of \eqref{eq:ar}.
Encoder and goal embedding construction are identical.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ODE one-step prediction (training \& CEM rollout)}
\label{alg:ode-step}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Context latents $Z$, action embeddings $A$, steps $N{=}4$
\State $z \gets Z$;\; $h \gets 1/N$
\For{$i = 1$ to $N$}
  \State $k_1 \gets v_\theta(z, A)$
  \State $k_2 \gets v_\theta(z + \tfrac{h}{2}k_1, A)$
  \State $k_3 \gets v_\theta(z + \tfrac{h}{2}k_2, A)$
  \State $k_4 \gets v_\theta(z + h\,k_3, A)$
  \State $z \gets z + \tfrac{h}{6}(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4)$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\hat Z_{t+1} \gets z$ \Comment{$z(1)$ after $\tau{:}0\to1$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Matched train / eval protocol (discrete vs.\ ODE)}
\label{alg:protocol}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Draw 1000 PushT windows with seed 3072; split 901/99
\State Train JEPA with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{JEPA}}+\lambda\mathrm{SIGReg}$
      for 100 epochs (AdamW, bf16, effective batch 128)
\State Select epoch-100 checkpoint
\State CEM on 93 feasible val windows (goal offset 25)
\State Report fit/loss, val/loss, and CEM successes / 93
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:impl}

The ODE predictor is implemented as a drop-in module with the same
\texttt{(x, c) -> pred} signature as the discrete AR predictor, so the
JEPA forward path, SIGReg hook, and CEM rollout loop need no API
changes beyond selecting the predictor class in the model config.
We implement fixed-step RK4 (and Euler) in-repo; training runs use
RK4 with $N{=}4$.
Velocity-head zero-init is applied once at construction.
On the RTX~4060 Laptop (8\,GiB), memory pressure forces
\texttt{num\_workers=0} and the 64$\times$accum-2 batch recipe for both
arms of the comparison, so wall-clock differences at train time are
dominated by the extra velocity evaluations inside RK4 rather than by
batch-size mismatch.
