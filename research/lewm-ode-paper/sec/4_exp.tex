\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
We use only the official Hugging Face PushT expert release
(\texttt{quentinll/lewm-pusht}, HDF5; no Cube / Two-rooms / Reacher
downloads in this study).
A reproducible subset of \textbf{1000} windows is drawn with seed
\textbf{3072} and split by \texttt{spt.random\_split([0.9, 0.1])} into
\textbf{901} train / \textbf{99} validation windows.
Because the validation fraction is passed as $1-0.9$ in floating
point, $\lfloor 1000\times 0.1\rfloor$ yields 99 validation indices and
the remainder assigns 901 to train---matching the frozen subset files
(\texttt{pusht\_expert\_train\_n1000\_seed3072} train/val index arrays).
Indices are never regenerated between discrete and ODE runs.
Of the 99 val windows, \textbf{93} remain feasible under
goal offset 25 for CEM (6 dropped because the goal frame would fall
past the episode end).

\paragraph{Models and compute.}
Both predictors use ViT-tiny, history 3, $D{=}192$, SIGReg
$\lambda{=}0.09$, AdamW lr $5{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay
$10^{-3}$, grad clip 1.0, bf16, 100 epochs, frameskip 5.
Hardware: RTX~4060 Laptop (8\,GiB).
Official batch size 128 OOMs with dataloader workers on this GPU; we
run batch 64 with gradient accumulation 2 (effective 128) and
\texttt{num\_workers=0}.
Parameter counts: discrete AR \textbf{18,034,478}; ODE
\textbf{18,071,534} (+37{,}056 from the velocity head).

Table~\ref{tab:results} summarizes the matched comparison.
The ODE predictor improves fit/loss (0.234 vs.\ 0.248) and val/loss
(0.506 vs.\ 0.536); final val prediction / SIGReg terms are
approximately 0.220 / 3.17 (\Cref{tab:ode-decomp}).
CEM success is identical: \textbf{5 successes out of 93} feasible
val starts ($\approx$5.38\%).
ODE CEM wall-clock is substantially higher ($\approx$82\,min /
4859\,s vs.\ $\approx$8\,min for discrete on the same machine) because
each rollout step runs four RK4 stages with four velocity evaluations
each.

\paragraph{Loss$\downarrow$ vs.\ CEM flat.}
Lower next-embedding loss does not yield higher CEM success under
this budget.
Several non-exclusive explanations are consistent with the data:
(i)~the 1k subset may be too small for planning-relevant long-horizon
structure to separate the two predictors;
(ii)~CEM hyperparameters (horizon 5, 300 samples) may be the bottleneck
rather than one-step prediction error;
(iii)~goal-offset feasibility and the sparse success events (only 5
hits) make the metric high-variance at $n{=}93$;
(iv)~RK4's extra compute does not change the discrete CEM interface,
so planning search explores the same action schedule.
We therefore report a \emph{tie} on CEM and a clear but modest win on
loss---not a planning breakthrough.

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
Figure~\ref{fig:curves-ar} and Figure~\ref{fig:curves-ode} show
that both predictors decrease fit and val loss over 100 epochs on the
same split.
The ODE run exhibits a transiently high validation SIGReg term in
early epochs (on the order of $30$--$100$) before settling near
$\approx 3.17$, comparable to the discrete baseline's late-training
SIGReg scale.
This suggests that the continuous residual path does not permanently
destabilize SIGReg under $\lambda{=}0.09$, but can delay isotropy early
in training when the velocity head leaves the zero-init regime.

\paragraph{Protocol note.}
Earlier CEM probes on random full-dataset starts (e.g.\ 0/10 for the
100-epoch discrete checkpoint, 1/10 for a 1-epoch smoke run) are
\emph{not} val-tied and are discarded; all claims here use the
val-99 / 93-feasible protocol shared with \texttt{validate/loss}.
The prior 0/10 result reflected a protocol mismatch (random windows
from the $\sim$1.9M pool), not total model collapse.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{1k subset / not paper scale.}
Results are on 1000 windows, not the full PushT expert corpus.
Paper-scale $\approx$96\% success is out of scope for this study;
closing that gap requires full-data 100-epoch training and
paper-protocol CEM ($n{=}50$), which we have not run.

\item \textbf{CEM tied.}
Lower loss does not yield higher CEM success under this budget;
planning may be bottlenecked by data scale, CEM hyperparameters, or
goal-offset feasibility rather than predictor form.

\item \textbf{Single environment.}
Only PushT is evaluated; Cube, Two-rooms, and Reacher remain future
work (official HF datasets exist but were not downloaded here).

\item \textbf{Fixed solver.}
We use RK4 with $N{=}4$ and no adaptive stepping; Euler is implemented
but not ablated at 100 epochs.
Broader ODE solvers, multi-step $\tau$ schedules, and
physical-time flow as in \PriorODEWorld{}~\cite{liu2026odeworld} are unexplored.

\item \textbf{Compute.}
ODE CEM is $\sim$10$\times$ slower than discrete AR on the same GPU;
any deployment story must weigh loss gains against planning latency.

\item \textbf{Single seed / single split.}
All numbers use seed 3072 and one frozen 901/99 split.
We do not report multi-seed confidence intervals; the 5/93 CEM count
should be read as a point estimate under this protocol.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The matched comparison supports three practical takeaways.

First, continuous latent dynamics are a \emph{compatible} inductive
bias for JEPA + SIGReg training: the ODE predictor trains stably
for 100 epochs under the same SIGReg weight, reaches lower fit/val
loss, and requires only a small parameter delta (+37{,}056).

Second, compatibility is not the same as a planning win.
CEM success remains 5/93 for both predictors.
In a low-data regime with a sparse binary success metric, improved
one-step embedding fit need not rearrange which of 93 starts are
solved under a fixed CEM budget.
We therefore treat loss and CEM as separate axes and refuse to conflate
them.

Third, protocol discipline matters as much as predictor choice.
Random-start CEM probes produced misleading 0/10 or 1/10 scores that
are not comparable across runs; the val-tied protocol is what makes
the discrete vs.\ ODE comparison legitimate.
Future scaling experiments should preserve the same discipline:
frozen splits, shared CEM starts, and explicit separation between
subset ablations and paper-scale claims.

\paragraph{What we are \emph{not} claiming.}
We do not claim paper-scale PushT performance, multi-seed statistical
significance of the 5/93 tie, superiority of ODEs for CEM in general,
or that lower JEPA loss implies better control.
We claim only a matched 1k comparison on official data: ODE reduces
reported train/val loss and matches discrete CEM success under the
val-99 protocol documented above.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Natural next steps are (1)~full PushT training with the same ODE
drop-in, (2)~multi-environment CEM under matched splits,
(3)~solver ablations ($N$, Euler vs.\ RK4, adaptive integrators),
(4)~evaluating official HF pretrained weights under the same val-99
CEM protocol as an external reference, and
(5)~stronger continuous-time formulations in the spirit of
\PriorODEWorld{}~\cite{liu2026odeworld} beyond a one-step RK4 map.
