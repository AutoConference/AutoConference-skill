\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

World models that plan in a compact latent space enable fast
model-predictive control without decoding pixels at every step
\cite{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2019planet}.
Classical latent dynamics models often predict the next state with a
discrete transition (RNN, RSSM, or transformer block), then hand the
rollout to a planner such as CEM or MPPI.
Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)
\cite{assran2023jepa} push the representation side further by
predicting future \emph{embeddings} rather than reconstructions,
avoiding a pixel decoder that can dominate compute and encourage
shortcut features.
LeWorldModel (\LeWM{})~\cite{maes2026lewm} shows that a stable
end-to-end JEPA can be trained from pixels with only two loss terms:
next-embedding MSE and SIGReg isotropic-Gaussian regularization
\cite{balestriero2023sigreg}.
The resulting system supports Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planning
directly in latent space and reports strong PushT success under
full-data training.

In \LeWM{}, the predictor is a discrete autoregressive transformer
block: given context latents $z_t$ and action embeddings $a_t$, it
directly outputs $\hat z_{t+1}$.
Physical dynamics, however, are continuous in time.
A discrete next-step map must absorb the entire transition into a
single nonlinear layer, with no explicit notion of intermediate flow
along the planning interval.
Recent continuous-time world models such as
\ODEWorld{}~\cite{liu2026odeworld} argue that latent velocity fields
$\mathrm{d}z/\mathrm{d}t = v_\theta(\cdot)$ better capture underlying
flow and support flexible temporal resolution.
Neural ODEs~\cite{chen2018neuralode} provide the standard tool for
parameterizing such fields and integrating them with classical
solvers.

This creates a natural inductive-bias question for planning JEPA
models: if the rest of the \LeWM{} stack---encoder, SIGReg, projectors,
CEM---is held fixed, does replacing the discrete AR predictor with an
integrate-to-next-step ODE improve representation quality and/or
planning success on official control data?

\subsection{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem}

We study a \emph{minimal} predictor swap under a matched experimental
protocol.
Let $f_\phi$ be the pixel encoder, $p$ and $q$ the projectors,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SIGReg}}$ the anti-collapse regularizer, and
$\mathrm{CEM}$ the official planner.
The discrete baseline predicts
\begin{equation}
  \hat Z_{t+1} = g^{\mathrm{AR}}_\theta(Z_t, A_t),
\end{equation}
while our continuous alternative defines a velocity field $v_\theta$
and
\begin{equation}
  \hat Z_{t+1}
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{ODESolve}\bigl(v_\theta;\, Z_t, A_t;\, \tau{:}0\to 1\bigr).
\end{equation}
Both models are trained with the same JEPA objective
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SIGReg}}$
on the same official PushT windows, and evaluated with the same
val-tied CEM starts.
We deliberately do \emph{not} redesign the encoder, change SIGReg
hyperparameters, introduce pixel reconstruction, or invent new
planning costs: any difference is attributable to the predictor
dynamics.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can we replace \LeWM{}'s discrete AR predictor with an ODE
integrate-to-next-step rule, keep the rest of the pipeline fixed, and
retain competitive CEM planning on official PushT data?}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{ODE drop-in predictor.}
We instantiate an action-conditioned ODE predictor inside the
official \LeWM{} JEPA:
$\mathrm{d}z/\mathrm{d}\tau = v_\theta(z,a)$, with one-step prediction
defined as classical RK4 integration over $\tau\in[0,1]$ (fixed
$N{=}4$ substeps), actions held constant on the interval, and a
zero-initialized linear velocity head for residual stability at
initialization.
The velocity backbone reuses the same ConditionalBlock / AdaLN-zero
transformer capacity as the discrete AR predictor
($\approx$18.07M parameters; +37{,}056 from the $v$-head).

\item \textbf{Matched 1k protocol on official data.}
We evaluate on the official Hugging Face PushT expert HDF5 with a
reproducible 1000-window split (seed~3072; 901 train / 99 val),
matching encoder, SIGReg ($\lambda{=}0.09$, $M{=}1024$), optimizer,
hardware batching (64$\times$accum~2), and CEM protocol between
discrete and ODE runs.
Indices are frozen and never regenerated between runs.

\item \textbf{Honest empirical findings.}
On this protocol, ODE lowers train/val loss relative to discrete AR
(0.234 / 0.506 vs.\ 0.248 / 0.536), while CEM success on the val-99
protocol is \textbf{tied} at 5/93~(5.38\%).
We explicitly contrast this with the paper-reported
$96{\pm}2.83\%$ PushT figure under full-data training, and treat the
1k regime as a controlled ablation rather than a claim of
paper-scale performance.
No synthetic episodes are used.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why this matters.}
If continuous dynamics help even as a drop-in, JEPA world models can
borrow ODE inductive bias without abandoning SIGReg or CEM.
If they do not help planning under matched compute and data, that is
also useful: it suggests that predictor form is secondary to data scale
and planner configuration in the 1k regime.
Our results land in the middle---loss improves, CEM does not---and we
report both sides without overselling.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This paper is a controlled predictor ablation, not a full re-training
of \LeWM{} at paper scale, and not a multi-environment bake-off.
Cube, Two-rooms, and Reacher remain future work
(\Cref{sec:limitations}).
We also do not convert or evaluate the official Hugging Face pretrained
PushT weights under our val-99 protocol; that baseline remains future
work.
